## The big idea (core promise)

**Hand me the whole website. I'll redesign it, host it, keep it updated — and you'll never
chase a team or sort through bids again. I'm one text away.**

Emotional payoff: *relief* + being treated like a person, not a ticket number.

## Who we're writing to

Expert-at-their-craft owners — contractors, consultants, wellness practices, nonprofits,
creatives, small professional firms. DMV-leaning, but the work spans niches (real clients in
DC, Portland, Warsaw). They're great at their actual job, underwater on "the web stuff,"
sitting on a dated site that quietly embarrasses them, short on time, and wary of agencies
(meetings, process, upcharges) and freelance marketplaces (bids, faceless, flaky).

**Crucial context — they already know me.** This site is reached directly or by referral, so
it's *trust-deepening for a warm lead*, not cold acquisition. Be personal; skip "why you need
a website 101"; go straight to who I am and why I'm the easy yes.

## Why Derrick (the wedge) — distilled from his own words

**Everything off your plate, through one human you can actually text.**

- **vs an agency:** no full team to meet with, no process theater, no nickel-and-diming every change.
- **vs Upwork / freelancers:** no posting jobs, no sorting bids, no faceless contractor — a known person who finishes.
- **vs DIY:** you don't learn any of it. Hosting, updates, design, *even the copy* — handled.
- **Human:** life happens. Reschedule, no shame. Need a solid (like copy written for you)? Done, without a crazy upcharge.

A real client already said the quiet part: *"…if I ever need anything I always know I can just
call him and the site will be updated in 48 hours."*

## Voice: warm, but authoritative

Confident enough to charge $5–15k; human enough to text back the same day. A seasoned
specialist who's done this hundreds of times *and* treats you like a neighbor — the trusted
family doctor / the GC everyone recommends. Expert **and** approachable.

**Do**
- First person ("I"), direct address ("you"), contractions.
- Plain language. Explain the nerdy stuff simply, or just say "I handle that."
- Decisive, confident verbs; own the outcome. (Authority = no hedging.)
- Concrete specifics over adjectives: "one text away," "updated in 48 hours," "I host it, I update it."
- Short paragraphs. Let warmth and the occasional dry aside through.
- Name the reader's real feelings (overwhelm, the embarrassing old site) — then relieve them.

**Don't**
- Jargon ("CMS," "responsive," "leverage," "solutions," "synergy").
- Agency "we" — it's "I." One person is the whole point.
- Hype, exclamation spam, or sales pressure (they already trust me).
- Hedging ("might," "we could maybe").
- Shame the reader for an old site, a tight budget, or a reschedule.
- **Never use the em dash (`—`).** Use a period, a comma, "so", or parentheses instead, or just split the sentence. (En dashes in number ranges like `$2,500–$3,500` are fine.)
- Other AI tells to avoid: the "not just X, it's Y" construction, stacked one-line fragments for drama, a tidy rule-of-three in every line, "in today's digital landscape." Read it back and make sure it sounds like a person typed it.

**Voice seeds (his own — reuse / adapt)**
- "get back to the work you're great at"
- "it's gonna get a bit nerdy — that part's on me"
- "I build websites for people" (+ the accessibility line: *"how does this load for a blind person?"*)
- "All you do is send your images and the words. I handle everything else."
